## The chain of findings, in the order they were discovered

### 1. The grading label manufactures its own edge (real, proven)

Every signal was scored on `forward_abnormal_return = r − (alpha + beta·r_bench)`,
with alpha/beta fit on a **trailing 252-day window**. A stock that has been
falling has negative fitted alpha, and the label *subtracts* it — crediting
the name with return it never earned, more so the longer the horizon. Proven
mechanically: `rho(label_gap, trailing_120d_return)` runs from −0.181 at 5
days to **−0.635 at 120 days**, exactly the predicted ramp.

**Consequence: every cell ever admitted by the gate (DSR/PBO/K=2 ladder) was
graded on a contaminated number.** None of it is disproven, but none of it is
proven either. See `[[quant-label-not-tradeable]]` memory.

**Fix used for the rest of the session:** rebuild forward returns from
`adj_close`, by trading-day offset from `t0` (never calendar days), plus a
SPY-hedged variant. This is what "tradeable label" means everywhere below.

### 2. The strategy has no per-name stock-picking ability (real, proven)

- `predicted_move` (what the engine ranks on) is a **cell average** — same
  event type, same horizon → identical score for every company. Across
  16,851 cluster-buy signals there were only 15 distinct values.
- ~20 candidate features (purchase size, insider seniority, CEO buying,
  momentum, routine-vs-opportunistic insider, volatility, liquidity) were
  extracted from Form 4 payloads and measured against the tradeable label.
  All the promising-looking ones (rho up to −0.30) were re-measured against
  the *contaminated* label and collapsed to ~0 against the tradeable one —
  same mechanism as #1.
- A combined ranker, feature-selected on 2016–2021 and scored once on
  2022–2026, **ranked backwards out of sample.**
- Concentration test: pushing the book from 50 names down to 3 monotonically
  **destroyed** returns (16.13% → 2.68% CAGR) while *raising* drawdown. That
  is the signature of zero per-name skill — concentrating into noise costs
  money.
- Measured beta sat at 0.92–1.05 across every slot count from 21 to 125
  names. **This portfolio is statistically indistinguishable from SPY with
  extra transaction costs.**

### 3. Loss-cutting rules worked exactly as designed, and still hurt in fast crashes

Investigated the March 2020 losers by request. The per-position stop and
loser-eviction machinery fired ~130 times in three weeks, exactly as
designed. It still produced −25% to −45% realized losses because:

- A daily-close stop is a **trigger, not a ceiling** — 100% of 279 stop exits
  in the full backtest realized worse than the 8% setting (mean −12.3%,
  worst −44.7%), because overnight gaps land wherever they land.
- Rotation only helps against *relative* underperformance. In a market-wide
  panic (COVID, −33.7% SPY in 33 days) the replacement name is falling too.
  86% of positions opened during the Feb–Apr 2020 window lost money.
- Cash stayed at ~0% the entire crash (by design — "never sit on cash"), so
  every freed dollar was redeployed straight into the falling market.
- Turning the 8% stop off entirely gave a *smaller* 2020 drawdown (−38.6%
  vs −40.9%) than keeping it. This is a systematic-risk problem; a per-name
  stop is the wrong tool for it. `max_beta`/`target_beta` exist in
  `src/market_intelligence/config.py` from the previous session's Phase 0
  work but were never wired into any of tonight's runs — that's the
  untested candidate fix.
- Market regime base rate, measured on our own 10-year panel: a 10%+
  drawdown about every 2 years, a 20%+ bear about every 5 years, 38% of all
  sessions spent below a prior peak.

### 4. The cluster-buy detector selects the WORST insider-purchase subset (real, proven, placebo-controlled)

This is the actual breakthrough of the night. Re-graded the **entire
corpus** (all event types, all horizons) on tradeable labels — 77% of cells
looked positive, including insider *sells* at t=29.7, which is impossible on
its face. Built a placebo control: for every event, draw a random OTHER
ticker trading the *same day*, hold it the *same* number of bars. That
isolates whether the specific event matters or whether the whole panel just
tilts upward (it does — median stock in the panel loses to SPY by −4.4% over
120 days; equities are right-skewed and the index is carried by its winners).

Result, hedged vs SPY, trimmed-mean edge over the same-day random-stock
placebo:

| cell | N | edge over placebo |
|---|---|---|
| individual insider purchases (`insider_transaction/P`), 120d | 10,809 | **+8.13%** |
| `insider_cluster_buy` (what the whole system traded), 120d | 7,077 | **−0.32%** |

The cluster detector (2+ insiders buying within a 5-day window) fires mostly
**after large declines** — the March 2020 loser post-mortem already showed
this (losing trades averaged −22% trailing 120-day return at entry). It was
systematically picking falling knives out of an otherwise-profitable
population. **The whole rotation/bench/stop machinery built over the prior
sessions was built on top of the one insider signal that doesn't work.**
See `[[quant-insider-purchase-vs-cluster.md]]` memory for full numbers.

### 5. But the individual-purchase edge does not survive real trading costs (the reason the loop stopped)

- The edge is short-dated (~3 weeks). Capturing it requires ~19x annual
  portfolio turnover at a 25-slot book.
- Measured actual spreads (Corwin-Schultz estimator) on the names the
  strategy would trade: median 36bps, **trade-weighted mean 118bps**. These
  are small/mid caps, not the 10bps large-cap assumption the backtest
  defaults to. Break-even round-trip cost is ~75bps.
- Grid search over slots/stop-level: **train→test rank correlation was
  −0.11.** Backtest-chosen parameters carry zero information about what will
  work going forward — stop tuning those.
- **The most important bug caught tonight:** the winning-looking
  configurations (equal-weight ranking, $308k ending equity, etc.) all had
  `predicted_move` set to a **constant**, which leaves the engine no
  tiebreak basis between same-day candidates other than raw database row
  order — which is non-deterministic in DuckDB. Five loads of the identical
  query returned ending equity from $25,559 to $66,109. **Every "win" from
  that stretch was one lucky draw, not a result**, caught by a Monte Carlo
  test (25 seeded runs of the identical config): mean CAGR 12.66% ± 5.73% vs
  SPY 15.01%, only 7/25 beat SPY, only 2/25 cleared SPY+7.5%.

**This is where the `/loop` session stopped itself** — further parameter
search couldn't help, since the dominant "variable" was an arbitrary sort
order.
